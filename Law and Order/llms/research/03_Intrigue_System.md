# Dwarf Fortress Intrigue System

## Overview

The intrigue system represents hidden social networks of villains, agents, and conspirators who coordinate complex plots across multiple locations. Unlike direct military threats, intrigue operates through deception, manipulation, and corruption to achieve villainous goals ranging from artifact theft to the overthrow of civilizations.

## Core Mechanics

### Agreement-Based Coordination

**Foundation**: The intrigue system operates through agreements between historical figures.

**How Agreements Work**:
1. Historical figure (villain/agent) identifies target for recruitment
2. Initiator reaches out to target through social contact
3. Uses persuasion, blackmail, or bribery to negotiate
4. If successful, agreement is formed
5. Agreement binds parties to coordinated action

**Agreement Types**:
- **Funding**: Providing resources for plots
- **Assassination**: Coordinated murder of target
- **Theft**: Stealing specific items
- **Sabotage**: Undermining organizations
- **Protection**: Shielding conspirators from justice
- **Intelligence**: Sharing information
- **Military Support**: Coordinating armed actions

**Tracking**: Historical figures maintain lists of:
- Schemes they want to execute
- Agreements they've made
- Relationships with co-conspirators
- Past successes and failures

### Historical Figure Networks

**Network Structure**:
- **Villains**: Central figures directing plots
- **Lieutenants**: Trusted subordinates managing operations
- **Agents**: Field operatives conducting infiltration
- **Assets**: Corrupted locals providing assistance
- **Cutouts**: Intermediaries providing distance from villains

**Connection Types**:
- **Direct Control**: Villain → Lieutenant relationship
- **Operational Management**: Lieutenant → Agent relationship
- **Asset Handling**: Agent → Corrupted Local relationship
- **Coordination**: Agent ↔ Agent peer relationships

**Information Flow**:
- Agents report intelligence to handlers
- Handlers consolidate information for villains
- Villains issue directives down chain
- Cutouts mask connections between levels

## Villain Types and Motivations

### Mundane Villains

**Criminal Leaders**: Organized crime bosses seeking:
- Wealth accumulation through theft and extortion
- Power expansion through territorial control
- Revenge against rivals and enemies
- Protection from justice through corruption

**Corrupt Officials**: Position holders abusing authority for:
- Personal enrichment through embezzlement
- Political power through blackmail networks
- Faction advancement through sabotage of rivals
- Ideological goals through policy manipulation

**Ambitious Nobles**: Aristocrats pursuing:
- Higher titles through intrigue against superiors
- Territorial expansion through diplomatic manipulation
- Dynastic advancement through arranged marriages/assassinations
- Historical legacy through dramatic achievements

### Supernatural Villains

**Necromancers**: Undead-raising sorcerers seeking:
- Immortality preservation
- Undead army building through battlefield raids
- Territory control through intimidation
- Artifact collection for power enhancement
- Revenge against living civilizations

**Demons**: Supernatural entities pursuing:
- Chaos and destruction for its own sake
- Mortal world influence through cults
- Artifact acquisition for unknown purposes
- Entertainment through manipulation of mortals

**Mummies**: Undead guardians with goals:
- Protecting tombs and artifacts from disturbance
- Punishing those who violated sacred spaces
- Maintaining ancient power structures
- Defending undead domains from living

### Motivational Drivers

**Personal Grievances**:
- Seeking revenge for past wrongs
- Settling old grudges
- Responding to perceived insults
- Correcting historical injustices (from their perspective)

**Ideological Commitments**:
- Advancing religious beliefs
- Promoting political philosophies
- Enforcing cultural values
- Preserving traditions

**Practical Goals**:
- Accumulating wealth and resources
- Gaining political power
- Achieving security and protection
- Acquiring specific items (artifacts)

**Personality Traits**:
- High ambition drives power-seeking
- Low empathy enables ruthless methods
- High greed motivates theft and embezzlement
- Paranoia inspires preemptive strikes

## Plot Types

### Artifact Theft Operations

**Standard Artifact Heist**:
1. **Intelligence Gathering**: Villain learns fortress possesses valuable artifact through:
   - Spy reports from previous visits
   - Information from drunk visitors
   - Historical records of artifact creation
   - Trade caravan gossip

2. **Agent Deployment**: Organization sends agent to fortress:
   - Agent uses false identity to avoid detection
   - Poses as regular visitor (merchant, entertainer, pilgrim)
   - May visit multiple times to avoid suspicion
   - Establishes presence and builds rapport

3. **Target Selection**: Agent identifies corruption target:
   - Looks for dwarf with access to artifact location
   - Assesses personality vulnerabilities
   - Evaluates social connections
   - Determines optimal manipulation approach

4. **Corruption Phase**: Agent converts fortress resident:
   - Uses appropriate technique (see Corruption Techniques section)
   - Multiple conversations over time build relationship
   - Success depends on skills and personalities
   - Drunk targets easier to corrupt

5. **Execution**: Corrupted dwarf steals artifact:
   - Waits for opportunity when unobserved
   - Takes artifact from display/storage
   - Passes item to agent handler
   - Agent departs fortress with stolen goods

6. **Aftermath**: Organization retains artifact:
   - May display in villain's lair
   - Could use in further plots
   - Might trade to other villains
   - Serves as trophy of success

**Detection Points**:
- Interrogating agent before corruption succeeds
- Catching corrupted dwarf in theft act
- Noticing artifact missing and investigating
- Interrogating corrupted dwarf after theft

### Assassination Plots

**Political Murder**:
1. Villain identifies target (noble, leader, threat)
2. Recruits assassin with combat skills or poison access
3. Coordinates timing with other plots (wars, coups)
4. Assassin strikes when target vulnerable
5. Creates power vacuum or removes obstacle

**Revenge Killing**:
1. Villain or agent seeks personal vengeance
2. May corrupt local to perform murder (distance from crime)
3. Murder staged to look like accident or frame others
4. Serves psychological satisfaction more than strategic value

**Serial Murder** (Vampires):
1. Vampire infiltrates fortress under false identity
2. Feeds on residents over time
3. Creates false accusations to deflect suspicion
4. Eventually discovered through investigation or evidence

### Sabotage Operations

**Economic Sabotage**:
- **Conspiracy to Slow Labor**: Corrupted workers deliberately underperform
- **Supply Destruction**: Burning food stores, contaminating water
- **Trade Disruption**: Attacking caravans, spreading false rumors
- **Production Interference**: Breaking tools, ruining materials

**Military Sabotage**:
- **Defense Weakening**: Breaking traps, opening gates
- **Supply Deprivation**: Destroying weapons and armor
- **Intelligence Leakage**: Revealing fortress defenses to enemies
- **Timing Coordination**: Sabotage coincides with siege

**Social Sabotage**:
- **Reputation Damage**: Spreading lies about fortress or leaders
- **Internal Conflict**: Creating grudges and disputes
- **Morale Destruction**: Causing stress through various means
- **Justice Corruption**: Turning officials against innocents

### War Initiation

**Diplomatic Manipulation**:
1. **Target Selection**: Villain identifies civilizations to pit against each other
2. **Official Corruption**:
   - Corrupt leaders of both civilizations
   - Corrupt military commanders
   - Corrupt diplomatic advisors
3. **Relation Degradation**:
   - Corrupted officials create diplomatic incidents
   - False intelligence spreads about threats
   - Border provocations staged
   - Historical grievances emphasized
4. **War Declaration**: Once relations deteriorate sufficiently, war begins
5. **Profiteering**: Villain benefits from:
   - Chaos allowing other operations
   - Weakening of both sides
   - Acquisition of battlefield resources
   - Political power in post-war situation

### Coup and Takeover

**Government Infiltration**:
1. Corrupt multiple position holders in government
2. Build network of compromised officials
3. Isolate loyal officials through false accusations
4. Position villain allies in key roles
5. Execute coordinated takeover or gradual control shift

**Methods**:
- **Corrupt Imprisonment**: Frame loyal officials on false charges
- **Blackmail Network**: Control officials through secrets
- **Economic Control**: Control officials through debt/bribery
- **Military Capture**: Control armed forces through corrupted commanders

### Hostage Operations

**Kidnapping for Ransom**:
1. Identify valuable target (noble, skilled dwarf)
2. Coordinate snatch operation
3. Hold captive at secure location
4. Demand ransom from family/fortress
5. May torture or sell if ransom not paid

**Leverage Hostages**:
- Force compliance with villain demands
- Extract secrets under threat
- Trade hostages between villain networks
- Sacrifice hostages to send messages

## Corruption Techniques

### Intimidation

**Method**: Use threats and fear to coerce compliance.

**Effective Against**:
- Low confidence dwarves
- Those with vulnerabilities (family, secrets)
- Dwarves already stressed or afraid

**Approach**:
- Demonstrate power through displays
- Make explicit threats against target or loved ones
- Create atmosphere of inevitability
- Offer protection in exchange for compliance

**Skill Dependencies**:
- Intimidator skill (agent)
- Bravery (target's resistance)
- Confidence vs anxiety (personality)

**Weaknesses**: Creates resentful assets who may betray when safe.

### Asserting Rank

**Method**: Use social hierarchy to demand obedience.

**Effective Against**:
- Lower status dwarves
- Those with high respect for authority
- Military-minded individuals

**Approach**:
- Emphasize agent's status/position
- Invoke duty and obligation
- Appeal to tradition and order
- Frame request as legitimate command

**Requirements**:
- Agent must have (or claim) higher social position
- Target must value hierarchical structures
- Cultural context must support rank deference

**Weaknesses**: Doesn't work on equals or skeptics of authority.

### Blackmail

**Method**: Threaten to reveal damaging secrets unless target complies.

**Requirements**:
- Agent must know target's secrets
- Secret must be damaging if revealed
- Target must value reputation/position

**Information Gathering**:
- Observation of suspicious behavior
- Interrogation of drunk targets
- Infiltration of private spaces
- Extraction from other corrupted assets

**Effective Secrets**:
- Criminal activity
- Shameful behaviors
- Forbidden relationships
- Hidden identities

**Psychological Impact**: Creates deep resentment but strong compliance due to ongoing threat.

### Flattery

**Method**: Appeal to target's ego and vanity.

**Effective Against**:
- High pride/vanity dwarves
- Those seeking recognition
- Ambitious individuals
- Insecure dwarves needing validation

**Approach**:
- Excessive praise of skills/accomplishments
- Recognition of underappreciated contributions
- Promises of fame and glory
- Appeals to self-image

**Skill Dependencies**:
- Flatterer skill (agent)
- Vanity vs humility (target personality)

**Advantages**: Creates willing assets who enjoy participation.

**Weaknesses**: Sophisticated or humble targets resist easily.

### Exploiting Religious Sympathies

**Method**: Use shared religious beliefs to build trust and obligation.

**Effective Against**:
- Deeply religious dwarves
- Those feeling isolated in beliefs
- Dwarves seeking spiritual community
- Religious fanatics

**Approach**:
- Claim shared faith (true or false)
- Frame plot as religiously justified
- Invoke divine will or destiny
- Offer spiritual rewards

**Requirements**:
- Agent must know target's religious beliefs
- Sufficient religious knowledge to be convincing
- Plot must be framable in religious terms

**Psychological Basis**: Religion provides powerful justification that overrides secular morality.

### Promising Revenge

**Method**: Offer to help target punish their enemies.

**Effective Against**:
- Dwarves with strong grudges
- Those who've been wronged
- Vengeful personalities
- Dwarves feeling powerless

**Approach**:
- Identify target's enemies through conversation
- Express sympathy for grievances
- Offer organizational resources for revenge
- Frame plot participation as step toward vengeance

**Advantages**: Highly motivating; creates actively engaged assets.

**Target Profile**: Works best on those with high anger, low forgiveness.

### Direct Bribery

**Method**: Offer material rewards for cooperation.

**Types of Bribes**:
- **Treasure**: Gold, gems, valuable items
- **Artifacts**: Powerful or prestigious objects
- **Positions**: Promised ranks or titles
- **Tribute**: Ongoing payments
- **Resources**: Food, materials, tools
- **Services**: Protection, assistance

**Effective Against**:
- Greedy dwarves
- Those in desperate circumstances
- Materialistic personalities
- Pragmatists without strong loyalty

**Advantages**:
- Simple and direct
- Scales with wealth
- Creates clear transactional relationship

**Disadvantages**:
- Requires resources
- Creates mercenary assets who may betray for better offer
- Target may take bribe then not comply

### Technique Selection

**Agent Assessment**:
1. Agent evaluates target personality
2. Identifies vulnerabilities and desires
3. Selects optimal technique(s)
4. May combine multiple approaches
5. Adjusts based on initial response

**Skill Importance**: Agent's **Intrigue skill** and **Judge of Intent** heavily influence technique selection accuracy and execution success.

**Multiple Attempts**: If initial technique fails, agent may try different approach in subsequent interactions.

## Infiltration Operations

### False Identity

**Name Fabrication**:
- Agents use pseudonyms to hide true identity
- May have multiple false names for different operations
- False names prevent tracking across locations
- Real identity only revealed through interrogation

**Background Construction**:
- False profession claims
- Fabricated home civilization
- Invented personal history
- Fake relationships and connections

**Maintaining Cover**:
- Consistent behavior matching false identity
- Avoiding situations that might expose truth
- Limiting interactions that could reveal inconsistencies
- Quick departure after operation completion

### Visitor Exploitation

**Access Methods**:
- **Merchants**: Join trade caravans for access
- **Entertainers**: Perform in taverns while conducting espionage
- **Pilgrims**: Religious visits provide cover
- **Diplomats**: Official capacity grants extensive access
- **Scholars**: Research visits allow extensive observation
- **Petitioners**: Request residency while planning

**Advantages of Visitor Status**:
- Legitimate presence avoids suspicion
- Freedom of movement within fortress
- Access to public areas and social spaces
- Opportunity to interact with many residents
- Can leave without raising alarms

**Extended Stays**: Agents may visit multiple times or extend stays to:
- Build trust and relationships
- Identify optimal targets for corruption
- Gather comprehensive intelligence
- Wait for ideal opportunity

### Intelligence Gathering

**Information Collection**:
- **Artifact Location**: Observing where valuables stored
- **Security Assessment**: Identifying guards, defenses, vulnerabilities
- **Personnel Profiling**: Noting skills, personalities, relationships of residents
- **Schedule Learning**: Understanding routines and patterns
- **Political Intelligence**: Discovering leadership dynamics and tensions

**Social Engineering**:
- **Tavern Conversations**: Drunk dwarves reveal secrets
- **Casual Socializing**: Building rapport reduces suspicion
- **Eavesdropping**: Listening to private conversations
- **Observation**: Watching behaviors and interactions
- **Question Plants**: Asking seemingly innocent questions that reveal key information

**Information Flow**:
1. Agent collects intelligence during infiltration
2. Information retained in agent's knowledge
3. Shared with handler/villain when agent departs
4. Used to plan future operations
5. May be traded to other villain networks

## Counter-Intelligence and Detection

### Investigation Methods

**Routine Interrogation**:
- **Best Practice**: Interrogate all new arrivals immediately
- **Visitors**: Check merchants, entertainers, pilgrims
- **Immigrants**: Screen all new residents
- **Returning Residents**: Check those who left and returned

**Interrogation Results**:
- True identity revealed if using pseudonym
- Organization membership exposed
- Other members named
- Ongoing plots discovered
- Past crimes confessed

**Strategic Timing**: Interrogate before agent can corrupt residents, preventing plots before they begin.

### Behavioral Indicators

**Suspicious Patterns**:
- Extended visitor stays without clear purpose
- Excessive socializing with specific residents
- Presence near valuable artifacts repeatedly
- Conversations ending when others approach
- Departures shortly after crimes occur

**Social Network Analysis**:
- Multiple residents befriending same visitor
- Behavioral changes after interacting with visitor
- Unusual gift-giving or resource sharing
- Secret meetings in private areas

### Law Enforcement Officials

**Spy Masters**: Civilizations employ dedicated counter-intelligence officers who:
- Gather evidence through witnesses
- Conduct surveillance of suspicious persons
- Coordinate interrogations
- Track villain networks across locations
- Share intelligence with allied civilizations

**Captain of the Guard**: In fortresses, serves counter-intelligence role:
- Interrogates suspects
- Reviews witness reports
- Connects cases to broader patterns
- Identifies organization members

**Intelligence Network**: Law enforcement builds knowledge of:
- Known villain identities
- Organization structures
- Common plots and methods
- Historical patterns
- Cooperation between villain groups

### Corruption Prevention

**Official Protection**:
- High social skills in officials makes corruption harder
- Strong personality traits (brave, not greedy) resist techniques
- Loyalist ideology reduces susceptibility
- Happy, satisfied officials less vulnerable

**Social Cohesion**:
- Strong fortress relationships create loyalty
- Satisfied residents less corruptible
- Cultural unity reduces faction exploitation
- Shared ideology provides resistance

**Environmental Factors**:
- Well-managed stress reduces desperation
- Adequate resources remove material motivation
- Fair justice system maintains trust
- Strong leadership inspires loyalty

### Handling Discovered Plots

**Agent Capture**:
- Imprison agent before plot execution
- Interrogate thoroughly for network information
- Consider execution vs imprisonment
- Use as intelligence source

**Asset Handling**:
- Corrupted residents more complex morally
- Consider rehabilitation vs punishment
- Interrogation reveals extent of involvement
- May inform on handlers if pressured

**Network Disruption**:
- Use captured intelligence to identify other members
- Systematic interrogation of revealed conspirators
- Breaking command chain prevents coordination
- May involve multiple civilizations in investigation

## Organizational Structure

### Villain Networks

**Leadership**:
- Central villain directs overall strategy
- May be distant from operational details
- Protected by layers of intermediaries
- Often holds legitimate position of power

**Operational Layer**:
- Lieutenants manage specific plot types or regions
- Coordinate between multiple agents
- Make tactical decisions within strategic framework
- Report success/failure to villain

**Field Layer**:
- Agents conduct infiltration and corruption
- Execute specific assigned tasks
- Report intelligence to handlers
- Limited knowledge of full organization (operational security)

**Asset Layer**:
- Corrupted locals provide assistance
- May not know organization's full scope
- Perform specific requested actions
- Most vulnerable to discovery

### Criminal Organizations

**Bandit Gangs**:
- Structure: Hierarchical with clear leadership
- Goals: Robbery, kidnapping, protection rackets
- Methods: Violence, intimidation, theft
- Territory: Control trade routes and wilderness areas

**Smuggling Rings**:
- Structure: Distributed network with multiple nodes
- Goals: Moving contraband, evading authorities
- Methods: Bribery, secrecy, legitimate cover businesses
- Reach: Span multiple civilizations

**Cult Networks**:
- Structure: Cell-based with minimal interconnection
- Goals: Advance religious/ideological agenda, prepare for supernatural
- Methods: Indoctrination, secrecy, ritual
- Recruitment: Target vulnerable, isolated individuals

### Inter-Organization Dynamics

**Cooperation**:
- Villain networks may coordinate on large operations
- Share intelligence on target vulnerabilities
- Trade assets and resources
- Combine capabilities for complex plots

**Competition**:
- Rival networks may conflict over territories
- Competition for same targets (artifacts, positions)
- Undermining competitor operations
- Direct conflict between villains

**Hierarchy**:
- Powerful villains may command lesser villain networks
- Supernatural villains often dominant over mundane
- Necromancers build subordinate networks of undead agents
- Demons manipulate mortal villains as pawns

## RimWorld Modding Implications

### Core Adaptable Mechanics

1. **Agreement System**: Pawns could form hidden agreements to coordinate illicit activities, tracked in social systems

2. **Corruption Mechanics**: Use RimWorld social interactions as basis for turning colonists into sleeper agents

3. **Multi-Stage Plots**: Implement infiltration→corruption→execution structure for hostile faction operations

4. **False Identity**: Visitors could use fake names, requiring investigation to reveal true nature

5. **Intelligence Gathering**: Implement information mechanics where visitors learn about colony resources and defenses

6. **Network Structures**: Track organization membership, allowing player to uncover broader conspiracies

### Adaptation Challenges

1. **Scale Mismatch**: DF has hundreds of historical figures across world; RimWorld has smaller cast

2. **Historical Depth**: DF tracks centuries of history; RimWorld colonies exist for limited time

3. **World Simulation**: DF simulates entire world of intrigue; RimWorld focuses on single colony

4. **Information Asymmetry**: DF hides information effectively; RimWorld players expect transparency

5. **Player Agency**: Balance between emergent intrigue and player feeling blindsided

### Recommended Implementations

**Visitor Infiltration System**:
- Hostile faction sends agent as "refugee" or "trader"
- Agent uses social interactions to befriend colonists
- Corruption based on social skills + relationship + personality
- Successful corruption creates sleeper agent
- Sleeper agent assists in future raid (opens doors, sabotages defenses, steals items)

**Interrogation Mechanics**:
- Warden can interrogate prisoners and suspicious visitors
- Success based on social skills vs target resistance
- Reveals true faction, hidden goals, other infiltrators
- Failed interrogation may result in death or corruption of warden

**Plot Detection**:
- Suspicious behavior creates "suspicion" meter
- High suspicion allows interrogation
- Finding evidence (intercepted communications, hidden items) provides clues
- Player pieces together conspiracy through investigation

**Organization Tracking**:
- Revealed through interrogations
- Shows network of connected agents
- Breaking one link reveals others
- Provides strategic intelligence on hostile faction

**Corruption Techniques as Social Interactions**:
- Bribery (offer silver, items)
- Intimidation (threaten pawn or loved ones)
- Ideological appeal (exploit beliefs)
- Blackmail (discover and exploit secrets)
- Friendship (long-term social relationship building)

**Staged Plot Execution**:
1. **Phase 1**: Agent arrival (legitimate reason)
2. **Phase 2**: Intelligence gathering (observe colony)
3. **Phase 3**: Target selection (identify vulnerability)
4. **Phase 4**: Corruption (turn colonist)
5. **Phase 5**: Coordination (wait for opportunity)
6. **Phase 6**: Execution (theft/sabotage/attack)
7. **Phase 7**: Escape (leave before discovered)

**Counter-Intelligence Gameplay**:
- Appoint security chief (interrogation specialist)
- Build interrogation room for skill bonuses
- Routine screening of arrivals
- Investigation of suspicious behaviors
- Network mapping when conspiracies discovered
- Decision: punish corrupted colonist or attempt rehabilitation

### Balancing Considerations

**Frequency**: Intrigue plots should be rare enough to feel special, common enough to justify counter-intelligence infrastructure

**Visibility**: Some clues should be noticeable; perfect deception frustrates players

**Consequences**: Failed detection should hurt but not destroy colony

**Recovery**: Players should have chance to recover from successful infiltration

**Skill Expression**: Both agent skills and security skills should matter, creating strategic depth

**Integration**: Intrigue should complement existing threat systems (raids, disasters) rather than replace them
