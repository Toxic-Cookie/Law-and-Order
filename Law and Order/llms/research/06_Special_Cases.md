# Dwarf Fortress Special Cases in Justice System

## Overview

Beyond standard criminal activity, Dwarf Fortress features several special cases that interact uniquely with the justice system. These include supernatural threats (vampires), stress-induced behaviors (tantrums and mental breakdowns), and systemic quirks that create emergent complexity.

## Vampires

### Nature and Characteristics

**Origin**: Vampires are dwarves (or other creatures) cursed during world generation for profaning against their former deities.

**Supernatural Abilities**:
- Immortal (don't age)
- Don't need food or drink
- Don't need sleep
- Enhanced physical attributes (strength, toughness)
- Rapid healing
- Can drain blood from unconscious victims

**Curse Compulsion**: Blood-drinking is not a choice but a supernatural compulsion. Vampires must feed to survive, making them inevitably dangerous to fortress populations.

### Identifying Vampires

**Skill Pattern Recognition**:
- **Key Indicator**: 10-15+ skills at Novice level or better
- Most dwarves have 3-5 skills from their profession
- Vampires accumulate skills over centuries of life
- Unusually broad skill set spanning unrelated professions

**Social Connection Anomalies**:
- Abnormally long lists of relations (50+ historical figures)
- Dozens of group associations spanning centuries
- Connections to people who died long ago
- Organizations that no longer exist

**Behavioral Patterns**:
- Never have thoughts about food or eating
- Never have thoughts about drinking
- Never have thoughts about sleeping
- No furniture-related thoughts (beds, dining rooms, chairs)
- These absences conspicuous compared to normal dwarves

**Physical Evidence**:
- Drained corpses found in fortress
- Living dwarves with severe blood loss but no memory of attack
- Pattern of deaths during sleeping hours
- Victims found pale and weak

**Combat Revelation**:
- If vampire fights in combat, true nature revealed
- Unit list shows "Urist McVampire, Vampire" instead of normal name
- Immediate confirmation of supernatural status

### Vampire Criminal Behavior

**Blood-Drinking as Murder**:
- Caught feeding = murder charge, even if victim survives
- Vampire won't stop drinking when caught
- Large happiness boost for vampire after feeding
- Victim has no memory if survives

**False Accusations**:
- Vampires typically file false witness reports
- Frame innocent dwarves for their murders
- Creates confusion and misdirection in investigations
- May lead to execution of innocents

**Frame Job Patterns**:
- Vampire makes report claiming to witness "murder"
- Blames dwarf with grudge relationship
- Or randomly selects scapegoat
- Justice system may convict based on vampire's testimony

**Serial Murder**:
- Vampires feed repeatedly over time
- Each feeding creates new murder case
- Pattern emerges of unexplained deaths
- Eventually investigation may reveal truth

### Justice System Inadequacy

**Standard Punishment Insufficient**:
- Vampires convicted of murder receive normal punishment
- Typical sentence: 8 months imprisonment or 50 hammer strikes
- Vampire's enhanced durability = high survival rate
- Released vampire returns to population, will feed again

**Imprisonment Problems**:
- Vampires don't need food/water (easy to serve time)
- Don't need sleep (no discomfort from poor accommodations)
- Enhanced toughness = resist stress better
- Time passes easily for immortal being

**Hammering Ineffectiveness**:
- Vampires survive 50+ hammer strikes regularly
- Rapid healing helps recovery
- May emerge injured but alive
- Returns to normal duties, remains dangerous

**System Cannot Execute**:
- No capital punishment in standard justice
- Hammering intended as corporal punishment, not execution
- Even if vampire dies, ghost may return to kill

### Extra-Judicial Handling

**Player Solutions**:

**"Accidents"**:
- Cave-ins that "happen" to kill vampire
- Drowning in "unfortunate" flooding
- Bridge raises at "coincidental" timing
- Falling down "poorly maintained" shafts

**Military Training**:
- Assign vampire to danger room
- "Training accident" occurs
- Spikes/blades kill vampire
- Appears as training mishap

**Permanent Imprisonment**:
- Imprison vampire without trial
- Never release
- Isolated cell prevents feeding
- Essentially life sentence without conviction

**Exile**:
- Force vampire out of fortress
- Remove from population
- Problem relocated rather than solved
- May return with sieges

**Arena Execution**:
- Place in arena with dangerous creatures
- Combat kills vampire
- Publicly visible justice
- Requires specialized infrastructure

**Ghost Prevention**:
- Ensure vampire body properly memorialized
- Build slab for vampire
- Engrave memorial
- Prevents ghost returning to kill residents

### Vampire Stress and Tantrums

**Clothing Degradation Problem**:
- Vampires live for centuries
- Clothes rot over time
- Naked vampires become severely stressed
- Eventually triggers tantrums or insanity

**Tantrum Consequences**:
- Violent outbursts
- Attacking other dwarves
- Destroying furniture and buildings
- May create additional criminal charges

**Prevention**:
- Provide clothing access to imprisoned vampires
- Regular clothing replacements
- Reduces stress-related problems
- Maintains some psychological stability

### Ethical and Moral Dimensions

**Curse vs Choice**:
- Vampires didn't choose vampirism
- Cursed by deity as punishment for ancient crime
- Blood-drinking compelled, not voluntary
- Raises question of moral culpability

**Public Safety vs Individual Justice**:
- Vampire will inevitably kill again
- Standard justice inadequate to protect fortress
- Extra-judicial methods necessary for public safety
- Tension between fair trial and pragmatic security

**Detection Difficulty**:
- Vampires use deception effectively
- False accusations deflect suspicion
- May live in fortress for years undetected
- Proactive investigation required

## Tantrums and Mental Breaks

### Mental Breakdown Types

**Tantrum** (High Anger Propensity):
- Cancel current job
- Throw items around
- Start fist fights with other dwarves
- Topple and destroy furniture
- Destroy buildings
- Attack pets and tame animals

**Melancholy** (High Depression Propensity):
- Cancel current job
- Mope around aimlessly
- Refuse food and drink
- Eventually die from self-neglect
- Non-violent, self-destructive

**Oblivious** (High Anxiety Propensity):
- Cancel current job
- Stumble around in confusion
- Unaware of surroundings
- Non-violent, non-destructive
- Eventually recovers naturally

**Berserk** (Extreme Stress):
- Attack anything in sight
- Kill other dwarves indiscriminately
- Destroy everything possible
- Requires military intervention
- Often ends in death

**Stark Raving Mad** (Permanent Insanity):
- Permanently mentally damaged
- Unpredictable behavior
- Alternates between calm and violent
- Never recovers
- Requires permanent management

### Stress Accumulation Mechanics

**Negative Stress Sources**:
- Witnessing death (especially friends/family)
- Working in terrible conditions (miasma, darkness, rain)
- Lack of basic needs (food, alcohol, sleep, social)
- Masterwork items destroyed
- Injuries and pain
- Confined spaces
- Lack of fulfillment
- Recent traumas

**Personality Amplification**:
- Stress-vulnerable traits magnify negative thoughts
- High stress_vulnerability = faster breakdown
- Low stress resistance = more severe reactions
- Personality determines breakdown type

**Threshold System**:
- Stress accumulates over time
- Reaches personality-dependent threshold
- Triggers mental breakdown
- Type determined by highest negative trait (anger/depression/anxiety)

### Criminal Behavior During Tantrums

**Tantrum-Related Crimes**:
- **Vandalism**: Toppling furniture
- **Building Destruction**: Destroying workshops, walls, bridges
- **Disorderly Conduct**: Attacking another dwarf (victim survives)
- **Murder**: Attacking another dwarf (victim dies)

**Legal Status**:
- Tantrum behaviors are crimes
- Witnesses report tantrum actions
- Justice system processes normally
- Conviction and punishment follow standard procedure

**Moral Complexity**:
- Dwarf not in control during tantrum
- Stress caused by fortress conditions
- Punishment may be unjust given diminished capacity
- Yet unpunished tantrums = danger to others

### Justice System Interaction

**Punishment During Stress**:
- Stressed dwarf commits crime
- Receives punishment (beating/imprisonment/hammering)
- Punishment increases stress further
- Increased stress may trigger another tantrum
- Cycle potentially repeats

**Tantrum Spiral Mechanism**:
1. Dwarf A stressed, throws tantrum
2. Tantrum crimes require punishment
3. Witnesses see harsh punishment
4. Witnesses become stressed from observation
5. Dwarf B throws tantrum from stress
6. More punishments required
7. More witnesses stressed
8. Dwarves C, D, E tantrum
9. Fortress spirals into chaos
10. Mass death or fortress collapse

**Breaking the Spiral**:
- Improve fortress conditions (reduce stress sources)
- Provide luxury accommodations (offset stress)
- Use minimal punishment (reduce witness stress)
- Isolate tantruming dwarves (prevent contagion)
- Emergency measures (alcohol, parties, break time)

### Exemption from Justice

**Strange Moods**:
- Dwarves in strange moods exempt from justice
- May commit technically criminal acts (claiming workshops, ignoring orders)
- No punishment possible during mood
- Behavior considered outside normal agency

**Permanent Insanity**:
- Stark raving mad dwarves exempt
- Not considered mentally competent
- Cannot be tried for crimes
- Require military handling if dangerous

**Children**:
- Children always exempt
- Tantruming children cause no criminal charges
- Recognized as not fully responsible

### Prevention and Management

**Stress Management**:
- Maintain good fortress conditions
- Provide quality food, drink, and accommodations
- Create recreation opportunities
- Offer variety and stimulation
- Satisfy individual preferences when possible

**Early Intervention**:
- Monitor stress levels
- Identify at-risk dwarves
- Provide luxury items to high-stress dwarves
- Temporary breaks from stressful duties
- Assign preferred labors

**Environmental Improvements**:
- Remove miasma sources
- Provide adequate lighting
- Create beautiful spaces (engravings, statues)
- Build temples for prayer
- Construct taverns for socializing
- Design impressive dining halls

**Personality Screening**:
- Avoid stress-vulnerable dwarves in high-stress roles
- Assign high-anger dwarves to low-frustration work
- Give cheerful, stress-resistant dwarves difficult duties
- Use personality traits strategically

## Systemic Edge Cases

### Impossible Accusations

**Dead Suspects**:
- Justice system may accuse dwarves who died before crime occurred
- Technically impossible for them to be guilty
- May be bug or historical figure tracking issue
- Creates obvious injustice

**Animal Suspects**:
- Tame animals sometimes accused of crimes
- Cannot be interrogated or punished
- Case remains perpetually open
- Source of fortress comedy/frustration

**Absent Suspects**:
- Creatures that were never in fortress accused
- Long-departed visitors blamed
- No way to apprehend or try
- Case unsolvable

**Population Reaction**:
- "Affronted at particularly nonsensical convictions"
- Morale penalty when obvious injustice occurs
- Recognition that system is flawed
- May reduce trust in justice overall

### False Witness Testimony

**Grudge-Based Lies**:
- Dwarf with personal grudge against another
- Files false report accusing enemy of crime
- Justice system may believe liar
- Innocent dwarf punished based on false testimony

**Motivation**:
- Personal conflicts
- Romantic rivalry
- Professional jealousy
- Historical grievances
- Personality conflicts (incompatible traits)

**Detection Difficulty**:
- No mechanical way to verify testimony
- Player must use contextual judgment
- Multiple witnesses provide more reliability
- Single witness testimony highly suspect

**Consequences**:
- Innocent dwarves punished
- True criminal remains free
- Justice system undermined
- Population becomes "affronted"

### Mandate Victim Selection

**Profession-Based Targeting**:
- Noble issues production mandate
- Mandate fails
- Noble prefers punishing dwarf whose profession relates to mandate
- Makes logical sense (punish carpenter for not making furniture)

**Random Victim Selection**:
- If no relevant professional exists, random dwarf selected
- Completely innocent dwarf punished for impossible task
- Creates obvious injustice
- Victim had no ability to prevent crime

**Systemic Injustice**:
- Some "criminals" are scapegoats for player/systemic failures
- Player forgot mandate, dwarf punished
- No materials available, dwarf punished anyway
- Fortress lacks workshop, dwarf blamed

### Export Ban Hauler Problem

**Hauler as Criminal**:
- Noble bans exporting steel items
- Player authorizes trading steel sword
- Hauler brings sword to trade depot
- Hauler convicted of crime (not player, not broker)

**Injustice**:
- Hauler following orders
- No way for hauler to know about ban
- No choice in what to haul
- Punished for obeying instructions

**Multiple Convictions**:
- If 10 haulers brought banned items, all 10 convicted
- Each treated as separate criminal
- Mass punishment for single player decision

**Player Responsibility**:
- Player made mistake
- Innocent dwarves suffer consequences
- Creates guilt and moral discomfort
- Highlights systemic injustice issues

## Noble-Specific Issues

### Punishment Exemption

**PUNISHMENT_EXEMPTION Tag**:
- Some position holders immune to justice
- Can commit crimes with impunity
- Create injustice and corruption potential
- No recourse for victims

**Affected Positions**:
- High-ranking nobles (barons, counts, dukes, monarchs)
- Certain administrative positions
- Guild masters in some cases

**Consequences**:
- Nobles can violate own mandates
- Can commit murder without punishment
- Absolute power without accountability
- May contribute to fortress instability

### Imprisoned Noble Problems

**Office Access**:
- Imprisoned nobles lose access to offices
- Cannot issue mandates while imprisoned
- Administrative functions cease
- May create governance gap

**Room Requirements**:
- Nobles have strict room quality requirements
- Prison cell must meet these requirements
- Failure = additional noble tantrums
- Requires luxury prison construction

**Diplomatic Consequences**:
- Punishing high nobles angers civilization
- May reduce trade
- May reduce immigration
- May trigger military intervention
- Risk of fortress being "assisted" by civilization military

### Noble Mandate Persistence Bug

**Issue**: Mandates remain active even after issuing noble is replaced.

**Problem Scenario**:
1. Baron issues mandate to produce crowns
2. Mandate not fulfilled
3. Baron dies/replaced
4. New baron has no crown interest
5. Old mandate still enforced
6. Dwarves punished for violating dead noble's mandate

**Impact**:
- Confusing for player
- Unjust punishment system
- No clear way to cancel old mandates
- Requires tracking which mandates still active

## Corrupted Justice Officials

### Captain of the Guard Corruption

**Vulnerability**:
- Captain can be corrupted during interrogations
- Skilled infiltrator may turn Captain during questioning
- Uses bribery, intimidation, or other techniques
- Corrupted Captain becomes agent of villain organization

**Compromised System**:
- Corrupted Captain protects infiltrators
- Provides false intelligence
- Undermines investigations
- Sabotages justice administration
- Enables criminal networks

**Detection**:
- Interrogations producing no results consistently
- Known criminals not being pursued
- Suspicious behavior from Captain
- Requires interrogating the Captain (if possible)

**Resolution**:
- Replace Captain (if player notices)
- Use military to handle justice temporarily
- Interrogate Captain to confirm (risky)
- May require extra-judicial handling

### Guard Corruption

**Possibility**: Regular guards may also be corrupted.

**Effects**:
- Delays in arresting criminals
- Reduced punishment effectiveness
- Information leakage to criminal networks
- Prisoners escaping mysteriously

**Less Impactful**: Individual guard corruption less catastrophic than Captain corruption.

## RimWorld Modding Implications

### Vampire Analogues

**Potential Implementations**:

1. **Bloodlust Gene/Trait**: Pawns with uncontrollable violent urges
   - Must satisfy violence need periodically
   - Attacks sleeping colonists
   - Enhanced combat abilities
   - Difficult to detect until crimes occur

2. **Addiction-Driven Crime**: Severe drug addiction causes theft/violence
   - Addicted pawns steal drugs
   - Withdrawal triggers violent outbreaks
   - Enhanced abilities when high
   - Difficult to rehabilitate

3. **Psychic Sensitivity Extreme**: Psychically sensitive pawns driven mad
   - Uncontrollable due to psychic pressure
   - Commits crimes during psychic episodes
   - Enhanced psychic abilities
   - Requires special management

**Adaptation Considerations**:
- RimWorld players more attached to pawns than DF players to dwarves
- Execution mechanics exist but players resist using them
- Provide non-lethal management options (cryptosleep, psychic dampeners)
- Balance supernatural threat with player agency

### Mental Break Integration

**Direct Translation**:
- RimWorld already has mental break system
- Violent breaks (berserk, tantrum) can become crimes
- Link justice system to mental health management
- Punishment affects mood, creating feedback loops

**Enhanced System**:
- Track whether crime was committed during break vs premeditated
- Reduced punishment for diminished capacity
- Mental health treatment as alternative to punishment
- Ideology determines how mental breaks viewed (sickness vs weakness)

**Tantrum Spiral Prevention**:
- Implement stress contagion from witnessing punishment
- Create mechanical feedback loop
- Require player attention to colony mood
- Balance punishment severity with colony stability

### False Accusation System

**Witness Reliability**:
- Base accuracy on social relationship
- Enemies more likely to file false reports
- Friends less likely to report at all
- Neutral parties most reliable

**Investigation Challenge**:
- Multiple witnesses provide corroboration
- Single witness testimony suspect
- Interrogation can reveal lies
- Player must weigh evidence quality

**Morale Consequences**:
- False convictions damage colony trust
- Correctly identifying false accusations builds warden reputation
- System creates detective gameplay layer

### Systemic Injustice Options

**Automatic vs Player Conviction**:
- Some crimes auto-convict (work violations, minor offenses)
- Serious crimes require player judgment
- Creates moral engagement
- Player must balance justice vs mercy

**Scapegoat Mechanics**:
- When true culprit unknown, system might pressure player to blame someone
- Leaders demanding "someone" be punished
- Player can resist or comply
- Moral weight to decision

**Noble/Leader Exemptions**:
- High-status colonists harder to prosecute
- Requires more evidence
- Faction leader cannot be imprisoned
- Creates political complexity

### Corrupted Officials

**Warden Corruption**:
- Enemy factions can turn warden
- Corrupted warden releases prisoners
- Sabotages investigations
- Provides false intelligence

**Detection**:
- Suspicious patterns (frequent escapes, unsolved cases)
- Can investigate warden through other colonists
- Requires replacement if confirmed
- Creates paranoia and interesting gameplay

**Prevention**:
- High-skill, high-mood wardens resist corruption
- Strong ideology resistance
- Multiple investigators provide redundancy
- Security culture reduces vulnerability

### Recommended Implementations

**Special Case Categories**:

1. **Compulsion Crimes** (vampire analogue):
   - Trait/gene causes uncontrollable behavior
   - Enhanced abilities offset danger
   - Requires special management (not standard punishment)
   - Player choice: manage, exile, or eliminate

2. **Diminished Capacity** (mental breaks):
   - Crimes during breaks treated differently
   - Reduced punishment severity
   - Mental health treatment as alternative
   - Prevents punishment spiral

3. **False Accusations** (social manipulation):
   - Relationship-based witness reliability
   - Investigation reveals truth
   - Wrong convictions damage colony trust
   - Detective gameplay layer

4. **Systemic Injustice** (mandate victims):
   - Auto-convictions sometimes target innocents
   - Player can override but faces pressure
   - Moral engagement with justice system
   - Highlights imperfection of automated justice

5. **Corrupted Justice** (infiltration):
   - Officials can be turned by enemies
   - Compromises system from within
   - Requires detection and replacement
   - Adds paranoia and political complexity

**Balance Goals**:
- Special cases should be rare but impactful
- Create emergent stories and moral dilemmas
- Avoid frustrating player excessively
- Provide player tools to handle situations
- Maintain strategic depth without overwhelming complexity
